# Source map

Use GOV.UK Service Manual guidance as the primary operational reference for continuous, decision-linked, inclusive user research. Use AAPOR when a survey or population claim introduces sampling, questionnaire, weighting, or uncertainty requirements. Community agent skills are workflow-pattern evidence only.

## Maintained boundaries

- Frame the decision and uncertainty before selecting a method. Existing evidence may answer the question more cheaply than new sessions.
- Qualitative methods can explain context, needs, behavior, mental models, and mechanisms; a small convenience sample does not establish prevalence.
- Testing whether representative people can complete a specific design belongs primarily to `usability-evaluation`. Controlled causal effects belong to experimentation/statistical practice. Behavioral telemetry belongs to product analytics. Market size and competitor demand research are not automatically user research.
- Recruitment should reflect differences capable of changing the decision, not generic demographic variety. Include disabled people and access needs when relevant, and do not let research logistics exclude the population being studied.
- Record consent and participant-data constraints before collection. Keep raw observations distinguishable from interpretations, implications, and later product decisions.

## Community pattern review

The MIT-licensed Rampstack `ux-research` skill has useful decision-first and method-routing patterns, plus a clear boundary between discovery research and usability testing. Do not import its numerical recruiting heuristics, incentive amounts, or claims that a fixed interview count is sufficient without task-specific evidence.
