# User research

Start from the decision and uncertainty, not from a favorite method. State what is already known, what is assumed, what would change the decision, and whether existing research, support evidence, telemetry, artifacts, or prior studies can answer the question before collecting anything new. Choose observation/contextual work, interviews, diary studies, surveys, artifact analysis, or mixed evidence to fit the claim.

Recruit for differences capable of changing the decision rather than generic demographic variety. Include disabled people/access needs when relevant and avoid research logistics that silently exclude the population of interest. Establish consent, recording, retention, access, and participant-data constraints before collection.

Prefer questions and observations about actual behavior, context, workarounds, decisions, and artifacts over leading solution validation or unsupported hypotheticals. When hypothetical questions are necessary, label their evidential limits. During synthesis, keep raw observation/quote, interpretation, implication, and product decision separate; preserve contradictions, disconfirming cases, and uncertainty.

A complete research plan includes the decision-linked research plan, a discussion or observation guide, and a reusable evidence-synthesis template. Define how each artifact carries consent, source identity, observation-to-finding traceability, contradictions, sample limits, and confidence so required deliverables do not collapse into an unsupported summary.

Do not turn small qualitative samples into prevalence claims or fabricate personas, quotes, consensus, or findings. Use `usability-evaluation` for task performance on a specific design; use quantitative/statistical practice when population estimates or causal effects are the claim.
