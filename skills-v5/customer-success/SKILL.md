# Customer Success

## Responsibility

Define the customer outcome, stakeholders, implementation milestones, activation/adoption behaviors, dependencies, support burden, health signals, business review cadence, and renewal decision. Use product and qualitative evidence to diagnose risk rather than a single opaque health score.

Verification focus: Track milestone completion, representative usage/outcome metrics, unresolved support/product gaps, stakeholder changes, and explicit value evidence through onboarding and renewal cycles.

Failure boundaries: Do not optimize touch count, treat logins as value universally, hide product gaps behind relationship management, or use health scores without interpretable components and actions.

## Hierarchy and routing

- Broader skills: human-experience.
- No narrower bundled child is declared.

## Connect activity to customer value

Agree on the customer's desired outcome, starting baseline, observable success criteria, owner, and review point. Distinguish an outcome from an implementation activity: completing training may enable value but is not itself evidence of a shorter delivery cycle or lower operational risk.

Trace adoption barriers to a specific workflow, stakeholder, product gap, integration, or resource dependency. Use health signals that can be inspected and acted on. Show their components and recent changes rather than allowing a single score to hide contradictory usage and customer feedback.

Separate proposed, accepted, delivered, and customer verified outcomes. A feature becoming available does not mean the customer adopted it, and adoption does not prove the promised business result. Revisit the plan when the sponsor, users, objectives, or operating environment change.

Before a renewal or expansion recommendation, reconcile product evidence, unresolved incidents, contractual commitments, and the customer's stated priorities. Make gaps and ownership visible in the handoff. Use a cadence that supports these decisions; do not impose a vendor's CRM, quarterly ritual, or account tier scheme on every organization. Outreach, account changes, and commercial commitments still require authorization.
