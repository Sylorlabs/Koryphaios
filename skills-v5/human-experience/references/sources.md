# Source map

Use sources in this order when guidance conflicts:

1. ISO 9241-210:2019 for the durable human-centred design lifecycle and interactive system scope.
2. Current GOV.UK Service Manual guidance for problem-first discovery, user evidence, whole journeys, and cross-channel seams.
3. Community design skills only for demonstrated workflow patterns; they do not define Kory routing, permissions, or universal aesthetics.

## Maintained interpretation

`human-experience` is the coordinating layer. It owns the end-to-end human outcome and the evidence/assumption boundary, then delegates method depth to narrower Kory skills. A visual, research, accessibility, content, IA, interaction, usability, or developer-experience task does not become a second copy of that specialty here.

Route unknown needs or behavior to `user-research`; behavior and state to `interaction-design`; concepts and findability to `information-architecture`; hierarchy and visual direction to `visual-interface-design`; labels, status, and recovery language to `content-error-design`; equitable use to `accessibility-practice`; observed task performance to `usability-evaluation`; and developer-user journeys to `developer-experience`.

ISO 9241-210 explicitly covers human-centred design activities across the life cycle of computer-based interactive systems and spans hardware as well as software. GOV.UK's service guidance reinforces solving the user's whole problem, understanding context beyond one transaction, and treating online/offline handoffs as part of the journey. These support a medium-neutral parent rather than a browser-first UX contract.

SecondSky's `frontend-design` and `design-review` skills are MIT-licensed community pattern sources. Their strongest reusable lessons here are to begin from purpose/audience/constraints and to make critique evidence-based and state-aware. Their web-specific checks and aesthetic prescriptions belong in narrower visual/web skills, not this parent.
