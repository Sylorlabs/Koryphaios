# Procurement Vendor Evaluation

## Responsibility

Define required outcomes, must-have constraints, users/operators, integration/data/security needs, service/support, implementation effort, total cost, contractual risks, portability, exit/migration, and vendor viability before scoring products. Weight criteria by consequence rather than feature count.

Verification focus: Use authoritative documentation, security/compliance evidence, referenceable customers where appropriate, hands-on trials with representative workflows, pricing/contract review, failure/support tests, and migration/exit analysis.

Failure boundaries: Do not award points for irrelevant features, accept roadmap promises as current capability, compare teaser pricing without total cost, or ignore data export and termination costs.

## Hierarchy and routing

- Broader skills: market-competitive-research.
- No narrower bundled child is declared.

## Make the comparison defensible

Separate disqualifying requirements from weighted preferences before reviewing proposals. Compare the same workload, service period, adoption assumptions, and contractual scope. A score cannot compensate for a missing mandatory data boundary or operational capability. Mark unknown evidence as unknown rather than assigning an optimistic score.

Before scoring, close a compact decision contract that states the decision and service period, the candidates and common workload, every mandatory gate, the approved cost ceiling, the evidence status for each gate, and the owner of each unresolved item. Mandatory gates include outcome fit, integration, data/security/privacy/residency, service/support, implementation feasibility, contractual risk, portability/exit, vendor viability, and affordability. A failed gate is ineligible. An unresolved mandatory gate or material unknown cost is a hold until evidence closes it; weighted preferences cannot offset either condition.

Trace each consequential claim to current documentation, contract language, an independent assessment, a customer reference, or a representative trial. Distinguish certification scope from the actual service and deployment under review. Investigate subproviders, access, incident responsibilities, support, and update obligations in proportion to their supply chain risk.

Build total cost against the approved budget envelope using the same dated assumptions for every candidate. Include usage, infrastructure, internal engineering/operations effort, implementation, migration, training, support, growth, minimums/overages, renewal, export, egress, termination, and transition cost. Use base plus credible adverse cases when variable usage, growth, renewal, or exit can change affordability. Treat a budget breach as a failed gate unless an authorized owner changes the envelope; do not silently fund the gap from speculative savings.

Exercise a meaningful failure, support request, or data export when the evaluation requires operational evidence. Identify which functions remain possible when the vendor is unavailable or the contract ends. Define the recommendation rule, the condition that forces hold/rejection, and the recovery or exit path before declaring a winner.

## Finish the contract within the execution budget

When the caller supplies required artifacts, acceptance checks, or a time/tool/output budget, finish the smallest contract-complete artifact set before adding optional narrative. Prefer one concise gate/TCO table, one decision rule, and representative success, negative, and recovery evidence over exhaustive prose. Map every material claim to supplied evidence or mark it unavailable, inferred, stale, or unresolved. Stop expanding research, scoring detail, or prose once the remaining budget is needed to complete required artifacts and verification. Re-open or otherwise check the required outputs before claiming completion.

Report the criterion weights, evidence gaps, sensitivity of rankings, rejected alternatives, and conditions of any recommendation. Route legal interpretation to contract analysis and the broader funding choice to business case analysis. Vendor selection does not grant authority to accept terms, purchase, or transfer customer data.
