# Procurement Vendor Evaluation

## Responsibility

Define required outcomes, must-have constraints, users/operators, integration/data/security needs, service/support, implementation effort, total cost, contractual risks, portability, exit/migration, and vendor viability before scoring products. Weight criteria by consequence rather than feature count.

Verification focus: Use authoritative documentation, security/compliance evidence, referenceable customers where appropriate, hands-on trials with representative workflows, pricing/contract review, failure/support tests, and migration/exit analysis.

Failure boundaries: Do not award points for irrelevant features, accept roadmap promises as current capability, compare teaser pricing without total cost, or ignore data export and termination costs.

## Hierarchy and routing

- Broader skills: market-competitive-research.
- No narrower bundled child is declared.

## Make the comparison defensible

Separate disqualifying requirements from weighted preferences before reviewing proposals. Compare the same workload, service period, adoption assumptions, and contractual scope. A score cannot compensate for a missing mandatory data boundary or operational capability. Mark unknown evidence as unknown rather than assigning an optimistic score.

Trace each consequential claim to current documentation, contract language, an independent assessment, a customer reference, or a representative trial. Distinguish certification scope from the actual service and deployment under review. Investigate subproviders, access, incident responsibilities, support, and update obligations in proportion to their supply chain risk.

Include implementation, migration, training, support, growth, renewal, export, and termination costs. Exercise a meaningful failure, support request, or data export when the evaluation requires operational evidence. Identify which functions remain possible when the vendor is unavailable or the contract ends.

Report the criterion weights, evidence gaps, sensitivity of rankings, rejected alternatives, and conditions of any recommendation. Route legal interpretation to contract analysis and the broader funding choice to business case analysis. Vendor selection does not grant authority to accept terms, purchase, or transfer customer data.
