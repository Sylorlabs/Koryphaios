# Project Program Management

## Responsibility

Translate objectives into measurable deliverables and work packages with owners, dependencies, decision gates, acceptance evidence, and realistic capacity. Identify critical path and uncertainty separately from task volume, maintain risk/decision logs, and replan when evidence changes.

Verification focus: Review milestone evidence, dependency readiness, risk trends, blocked ownership, forecast accuracy, and acceptance criteria at each decision point; close work only when downstream consumers can use the result.

Failure boundaries: Do not use percent-complete theater, treat every task as equal priority, hide unresolved dependencies inside dates, or optimize schedule by removing verification/recovery work invisibly.

## Hierarchy and routing

- Broader skills: planning.
- No narrower bundled child is declared.

## Control dependencies and decisions

Define usable deliverables and their acceptance owners before organizing work. Connect activities by actual prerequisites, lead times, scarce capacity, and external decisions. Show assumptions separately from committed dates. A task count or percentage complete does not reveal whether the critical dependency can finish.

Maintain a coherent baseline and a current forecast. When evidence changes, identify which dependencies, resources, costs, or downstream commitments change with it. Record the decision and its reason instead of silently moving a deadline. Distinguish remaining effort from elapsed duration and availability.

Track risks by trigger, consequence, owner, response, and review point. Include integration, acceptance, recovery, and operational handoff in the plan. Escalate a dependency without a responsible owner rather than hiding it in a generic risk list. Do not recover a schedule by removing verification work without an explicit tradeoff decision.

At review, inspect completed deliverables, blocked prerequisites, forecast error, and whether the recipient can actually use the result. Use the smallest cadence and artifact set that preserves those decisions. Investment appraisal is separate from delivery management, and no particular scheduling product or ceremonial framework is mandatory.
