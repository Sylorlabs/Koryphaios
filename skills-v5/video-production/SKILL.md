# Video Production

## Responsibility

Define audience, outcome, duration, platform/aspect, narrative beats, shot grammar, pacing, visual continuity, motion language, voice/music/SFX, captions, and brand constraints. Build timing from the message and audio rather than decorating a sequence with arbitrary transitions.

Verification focus: Review full renders plus key frames for pacing, cuts, legibility, caption timing, audio levels, clipping, motion continuity, encoding, and target-platform dimensions; test beginning/end and seek points explicitly.

Failure boundaries: Do not overuse motion presets, let captions obscure primary content, rely on draft-preview timing as final render proof, or use copyrighted media without appropriate rights.

## Verify the timeline and final file

Build the timeline around the message, source footage, and audio. Establish frame rate, timebase, duration, aspect ratio, safe text area, caption language, and delivery requirements before detailed animation. Distinguish source timing from composition timing so trims, speed changes, and transitions do not silently shift speech or captions.

Preserve continuity across cuts and use motion to explain priority or change, not to fill every interval. Check caption accuracy, reading time, contrast, occlusion, and alignment with the spoken content. Keep music and effects subordinate to intelligibility where speech carries the message.

Render the deliverable and inspect the resulting streams, not only the project preview. Confirm expected video and audio tracks, dimensions, duration, frame rate, channel layout, and the chosen encoding. Metadata inspection can identify format mistakes but cannot judge pacing, lip sync, artifacts, or comfortable motion.

Watch the complete final render and inspect its opening, transitions, seek points, and ending. Listen for clipping, unintended silence, abrupt fades, and balance on representative playback. Preserve rights and provenance for supplied or generated media. The skill does not require one editor, fixed transition style, codec, or programming framework when another meets the delivery contract.
