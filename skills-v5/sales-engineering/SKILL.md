# Sales Engineering

## Responsibility

Start with customer outcomes, environment, current workflow, stakeholders, decision criteria, constraints, security/integration needs, and timeline. Map product capability honestly to those needs, build demos/prototypes using real boundaries, and record gaps/assumptions for product and delivery teams.

Verification focus: Rehearse the exact customer scenario, validate integration/security claims with authoritative evidence, capture technical acceptance criteria, and hand off configuration, gaps, commitments, and ownership explicitly.

Failure boundaries: Do not invent roadmap commitments, fake integrations/data as production capability, oversell unsupported scale/security, or run generic feature tours disconnected from customer decisions.

## Hierarchy and routing

- Broader skills: technical-communication.
- No narrower bundled child is declared.

## Design a discriminating evaluation

Turn discovery into a small set of customer accepted technical and business criteria. Identify the decision owner, users, environment, required integration, data access, constraints, and consequences of doing nothing. Distinguish a feature demonstration from a proof of value, and a proof of value from production delivery.

Choose a representative customer workflow and state which parts are real, simulated, mocked, or unavailable. Rehearse the path including an important failure or recovery case. A successful demonstration with synthetic data cannot establish the customer's scale, security, compliance, or operating cost.

Bound the evaluation by scope, access, success evidence, responsibilities, and a stop or next decision. Resolve prerequisites before promising dates. Record product gaps and roadmap requests without presenting uncommitted work as a capability. Customer credentials and data stay within the agreed environment.

Close with the observed criteria, unresolved risks, customer feedback, and an explicit delivery handoff. Include configuration, ownership, commitments, and assumptions that must be revalidated. The technical communication parent governs accurate explanation; this child owns a customer specific buying evaluation, not a compulsory sales methodology or CRM workflow.
